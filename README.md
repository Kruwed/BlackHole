# Black Hole

An open-source, non-custodial, privacy-focused Bitcoin wallet for the Linux desktop that asks only your own Bitcoin node about the blockchain.

Black Hole is a fork of [Wasabi Wallet](https://github.com/WalletWasabi/WalletWasabi). It keeps the Tor integration and the WabiSabi coinjoins of Wasabi Wallet and drops most of the rest.

## What it does

- **One wallet.** Its backup is SLIP39: 20 recovery words by default, or several shares with a threshold if you choose to split it. Before the wallet is created you confirm the backup that you wrote down, by putting every word of it into its place, of every share; no word is filled in for you, and the confirmation cannot be skipped. The password encrypts the wallet file on the computer only; the backup alone recovers the wallet.
- **Taproot only.** Every address of the wallet is a Taproot (P2TR) address.
- **A new address for every payment.** *Receive* lists the addresses that await a payment, and *New Address* there generates one that was not given out before. It is in the dialog that *Coinjoin* on the *Coins* tab opens.
- **Your node only.** Blocks, unconfirmed transactions and fee estimations come from the RPC interface of your Bitcoin node, and transactions leave through the private broadcast of the node, see [Private broadcast](#private-broadcast). The wallet scans the blocks of the node, starting with the block the wallet was created at.
- **Coinjoins** with the coordinator that you choose, over Tor: WabiSabi rounds, and ZeroLink rounds, which take one coin of a standard amount from every participant and give one output each. See [Coinjoin](#coinjoin) and [ZeroLink rounds](#zerolink-rounds).
- **Manual coin control**: you tick the coins that a payment spends and that a coinjoin round gets. A maker ticks the coins of a bond and of an offer in the list of its own tab.
- **Fidelity bonds**: coins that are locked for a number of blocks that you name. See [Fidelity bonds](#fidelity-bonds).
- **A Nostr relay of your own**: Black Hole can run a relay that is reached at an onion address and nowhere else, for the offers of the market or for the kinds of events that you name. See [Your own Nostr relay](#your-own-nostr-relay).
- **A market for anonymity points**: as a maker you lock a bond and post offers to Nostr relays over Tor. As a taker you read the offers of others, filter them, pick makers and see the transaction that they would make with your coins. That transaction cannot be made yet. See [The market for anonymity points](#the-market-for-anonymity-points).

## The settings

The gear in the top right corner of the window opens the settings. They have five columns, and every column has two boxes. In the first four the upper one connects to something that exists and the lower one is for running your own. In the last the upper one creates the wallet and the lower one recovers one.

| *Tor* | *Node* | *Nostr* | *Coordinator* | *Wallet* |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Connect to Tor | Connect to Node | Connect to Nostr | Connect to Coordinator | Create a Wallet |
| Run a Tor Relay | Run a Bitcoin Node | Run a Nostr Relay | Run a Coinjoin Coordinator | Recover a Wallet |

*Run a Nostr Relay* runs a relay inside Black Hole, see [Your own Nostr relay](#your-own-nostr-relay). The other three *Run a …* boxes hold their place and do nothing yet. Each *Connect to …* box opens a page with two buttons:

- **Verify Connection** asks what the page names whether it answers, and tells the answer. It changes nothing.
- **Done** does the same and, if it answers, makes Black Hole work with it from then on, without a restart, saves it as the setting, and closes the page. Something that does not answer is not taken: the page stays open and tells why, and Black Hole keeps what it had.

What the pages are about:

- **Connect to Tor** asks for nothing. Black Hole starts its Tor when it starts; *Verify Connection* tells whether that Tor has connected to the Tor network and with how many unique Tor IP addresses, and *Done* waits until it has connected. The addresses are those of the relays that the circuits of Tor go through at that moment: first hops, middle relays and exits, every address once. The number changes as Tor opens and closes circuits. A Tor that Black Hole did not start (*Connect Only*) is not asked for its circuits, and the answer has no number then. The page also has how Tor is used (*Enabled*; *Connect Only*, which uses a Tor that runs on the computer already; *Disabled*) and whether Tor is stopped when Black Hole closes. These two take a restart. With Tor turned off, and on regtest, where Tor is not used, there is nothing to connect to: *Verify Connection* cannot be pressed, and *Done* closes the page.
- **Connect to Node** asks for the RPC endpoint and the RPC credentials of your node, see [Connect it to your node](#connect-it-to-your-node). The node has to answer and to be on the right network. The page also has the network, which takes a restart.
- **Connect to Nostr** asks for the relays, one address on a line. A connection is opened to each of them and closed again; nothing is sent to a relay. Nostr answers if one relay does, and after *Done* Black Hole posts to and reads from all the relays that you named, also those that did not answer this time.
- **Connect to Coordinator** asks for the address of the coordinator of your coinjoins, and asks the coordinator for its rounds. While a coinjoin is under way the coordinator is not changed: stop the coinjoin first. The page also has the highest mining fee rate and the lowest number of inputs that you accept of a round, which take a restart, and the dust forfeit limit of the wallet, see [Coinjoin](#coinjoin).

A node, relays or a coordinator that were given on the command line are shown and asked, and cannot be changed on the page. When a setting was changed that takes a restart, the settings say so and offer the restart.

**The marks.** A *Connect to …* box has an orange check mark if you have connected Black Hole to it and it works at the moment, and an orange X if you have and it does not. A box without a mark is one that you have not connected to, or a Tor that is not used. Whether the connections work is found out while the settings are in sight, and not otherwise: Tor and the node are looked at every few seconds, and the relays and the coordinator are asked when the settings open and then once a minute, the way *Verify Connection* asks them. Right after the start, while Tor is still connecting, nothing is told yet about Tor and about what is reached through it. *Run a Nostr Relay* has the same marks for your own relay: none while it is off or on its way, the check mark while it runs, the X while it is on and does not run.

**The wallet.** There is one wallet at most, so it is either created or recovered. Once it is there, the box that made it is *Configure Wallet*, with a check mark: the upper one if the wallet was created, the lower one if it was recovered. The other box stays what it was and can no longer be pressed. *Configure Wallet* has *Resync Wallet* and *Wallet Keys*, the extended public keys of the wallet. The wallet file tells whether the wallet was recovered; a file from before it did counts as created.

### The first start

As long as there is no wallet, the settings are the welcome. They cannot be put away, and the columns are set up from left to right: the boxes of a column can be pressed once the columns before it are done, and the column whose turn it is stands out. A column is done once you have connected on its page, which takes that Tor has connected to the Tor network, that the node answered, that a relay answered, that the coordinator answered. A Tor that is not used is done as it is. The wallet comes last, so there is no wallet before the four were connected.

Black Hole keeps note of what you have connected to (`ConfiguredConnections` in the config file), so a start without a wallet goes on where you were. A column stays done if what you connected to stops working: its box has the X then, and its page is where to connect again. A data folder that has a wallet needs no note: there, what the settings name counts as connected.

### Your own Nostr relay

*Run a Nostr Relay* opens the page of a relay that runs inside Black Hole for as long as Black Hole runs. It is off until you turn it on.

- **Nostr relay**: the switch. *Done* makes the relay what the page says, saves that, and closes the page once the relay runs, or is off. A relay that is on starts with Black Hole from then on. If it does not run yet, the page stays open and tells how far it is or why it cannot run, and the relay stays on and goes on trying.
- **Events**: which events the relay takes. You switch between two sets:
  - *Black Hole offers and coordinators*: the offers of [the market for anonymity points](#the-market-for-anonymity-points) and the announcements of coordinators, for the network of your wallet, and nothing else. Both are events of kind 15750. The relay forgets an offer 15 minutes after it was made, which is for how long an offer counts, and an announcement after 30. It keeps them in memory only: nothing that a stranger sends is written to your disk.
  - *Custom list*: every event of the kinds that you name under *Event kinds*, from anybody. The relay keeps these in a file, so they are still there after a restart, up to 100 MB or 200,000 events. Then the oldest events make way for new ones.
- **Event kinds**: the kinds of the custom list, as numbers and spans of numbers, like `1, 7, 30000-39999`. A kind is the number that tells what an event is: 1 is a short note, 0 a profile. The list is kept while the relay takes the other set.
- **Relay address**: the address of the relay while it runs, `ws://` followed by an onion address. Whoever is to use the relay is given this address; in Black Hole it goes into *Connect to Nostr*. Nobody is told the address by Black Hole, and your own Black Hole posts to your relay only if you name it there as well.
- **Verify Connection** asks the relay at its onion address, through Tor, the way anybody else reaches it.

**Reached at its onion address only.** The relay listens on this computer only (`127.0.0.1`, on a port that is free), where nothing from outside reaches it. Black Hole asks its Tor for an onion address that leads there, so whoever uses the relay comes through Tor and does not learn the address of your computer. The relay answers nobody who asks for another host than its onion address, which keeps out a page in your browser that tries the ports of your computer. It opens no connection of its own to anywhere. The onion address is gone when Black Hole closes, also if Tor is left running.

**What it takes.** Tor, started by Black Hole itself (*Enabled* on *Connect to Tor*): only that Tor can be asked for an onion address. With *Connect Only*, with Tor turned off, and on regtest, where Tor is not used, no relay can run, and the page says so.

**The address stays the same.** It follows from a key that Tor makes the first time and that Black Hole keeps in its data folder, in `NostrRelay/<network>/OnionServiceKey`. Whoever has that file can run a relay at your address. Delete it while the relay is off to get a new address. The events of a custom list are in `Events.sqlite` beside it; a kind that you take off the list is no longer handed out, and what the file has of it stays there.

**What it speaks.** The basic protocol of Nostr (NIP-01: events, subscriptions with filters, replaceable and addressable kinds, ephemeral events) and the description of itself that clients ask for (NIP-11). It does not act on deletion requests or expiry times, and it has no authentication. A relay of the first set calls itself "Black Hole relay"; a relay of a custom list calls itself "Nostr relay" and does not tell which program runs it.

**Its limits.** Anybody who knows the address can connect, and nothing tells one visitor from another, so everything that a visitor can make the relay do has a limit:

| | *Black Hole offers and coordinators* | *Custom list* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitors at once | 64 | 64 |
| Size of a message, which an event is | 16 KB | 64 KB |
| Messages of a visitor | 30 in a row, then 1 per second | 100 in a row, then 5 per second |
| Events of a visitor | 10 in a row, then 1 in 10 seconds | 50 in a row, then 1 per second |
| Subscriptions of a visitor, and filters of a subscription | 8 and 4 | 20 and 10 |
| Stored events in the answer to a filter | 2,000 | 500 |
| Events that the relay keeps | 5,000 | 200,000, and 100 MB |

When as many visitors are connected as may be, the one that was silent the longest makes way for a new one. A visitor that goes on beyond its pace, or that does not read what it subscribed to, is disconnected. When a relay of the first set holds 5,000 events, new ones are refused until old ones have expired, and what is there stays; an offer that is posted again takes the place of the one before and needs no new room. Tor guards the address as well: one circuit of a visitor holds 4 connections at most, and once more than 5 visitors per second come, Tor makes them solve a puzzle for their turn and lets those in first who put more work into it.

The relay is a setting like the others: `--runnostrrelay=true`, `--nostrrelayevents=Custom` and `--nostrrelaykinds="1;30023"` give it on the command line, and what was given there cannot be changed on the page.

## The window

The title bar lists the pages of the wallet as tabs, once the wallet is decrypted:

- **History**: the transactions of the wallet. The wallet opens with it.
- **Coins**: the coins of the wallet. Tick coins in the list and press *Coinjoin*: everything that is done with the ticked coins starts in the dialog that it opens, and so does receiving. The dialog has a tab for every protocol, and it shows the main screen of the tab that is open:
  - *Payjoin*, which the dialog opens with. Payjoin itself holds its place and does nothing yet; its main screen has the ordinary payment and *Receive*. A payment takes the address, the amount and the fee rate, and *Send* shows the transaction; nothing is sent before you confirm it there. The fee rate starts as what your node estimates for a confirmation within two blocks, and you can type another one. What you typed is still there when you open the dialog again, until the payment is sent. *Receive* opens the addresses that were generated and have not been paid to yet, with *New Address* for another one; a right click on an address copies it.
  - *WabiSabi*: *Play* starts one round with the ticked coins, see [Coinjoin](#coinjoin). Its main screen has the payments that wait for a round to make them, and *Add Payment* queues one.
  - *ZeroLink*: *Play* starts one round with one ticked coin of a standard amount, see [ZeroLink rounds](#zerolink-rounds).
  - *Taker*: the taker's side of [the market for anonymity points](#the-market-for-anonymity-points): the order book with its filters, and the transaction that the makers you pick would make with the ticked coins. Its *Play* cannot be pressed: the transaction cannot be made yet.
  - *Maker*: the maker's side of the market: the coins of the wallet, *Lock Coins* for a [fidelity bond](#fidelity-bonds) and *List Offers* for offers. The coins of a bond or of an offer are ticked in the list of this tab, not on the *Coins* tab.

  The dialog is larger while *Taker* or *Maker* is open: they have three columns each.

  Below the tabs is the box of the coinjoins, with *Play* and *Stop*: it shows the tab that is open. A wallet has one coinjoin at a time, and the box is where it is watched and stopped: while one is under way the box shows it, whatever tab is open, so that you can pay or receive while a round goes on. The coinjoin goes on when the dialog is closed, and the box shows it when the dialog is opened again.

  No coin is ticked until you tick it, and the ticks are not kept: after a start of Black Hole none is ticked. A payment that was sent unticks what was ticked for it. While the wallet still processes the blocks of your node, *Receive* is all that works in the dialog.
- **Order Book**: the makers of [the market for anonymity points](#the-market-for-anonymity-points) with what they offer, as your Nostr relays tell it: the makers with a fidelity bond first, then the others. The offers of this wallet are among them, with a *you* tag. It is the book to look at; makers are picked on the *Taker* tab. The relays are asked while the tab shows, at once and then every minute.

To the right of the tabs the title bar tells the fee rate, how many blocks the wallet has processed and the balance. In a window that is too narrow for all of it these give way, the fee rate first and the balance last, so that the tabs and the buttons stay. The gear at the right end opens [the settings](#the-settings).

## What was removed from Wasabi Wallet

Windows and macOS support, SegWit v0 addresses, hardware wallets, passphrases, BIP39 seed phrases, fiat exchange rates, the Branch and Bound search for changeless transactions, the dust attack limit, the Scheme scripting language, multiple wallets, confirmation time estimations, labels, the "ready to sign" signalling of coinjoins, the coinjoin strategies, the stop coinjoin threshold, the automatic start of coinjoins and their going on from round to round, the random and score-driven choice of the coins for a round with its isolation of non-private coins, the privacy progress with its ring, the anonymity score and its target, the sorting of the coins into private, semi-private and non-private ones, the privacy warnings of the transaction preview, compact block filter synchronization together with the peer to peer network stack, and every third party service (fee and exchange rate providers, block explorers, update checks).

## Requirements

- Linux on x64 or arm64.
- A Bitcoin node with the RPC interface enabled that broadcasts privately: Bitcoin Core 31 or later with `server=1` and `privatebroadcast=1`, and with a way to Tor or I2P, which the private broadcast goes through. See [Private broadcast](#private-broadcast). The node must have the blocks since the creation of the wallet, so a pruned node only works while it still has them.
- [.NET 10.0 SDK](https://dotnet.microsoft.com/download) to build from source.

## Build and run

```sh
git clone https://github.com/Kruwed/BlackHole.git
cd BlackHole/WalletWasabi.Fluent.Desktop
dotnet run
```

`WalletWasabi.Daemon` is the same wallet without the user interface; see its [README](WalletWasabi.Daemon/README.md). The names of the projects and of the namespaces are the ones of Wasabi Wallet.

## Connect it to your node

A Bitcoin Core on the same computer that uses its default data folder and its default RPC port is found without any configuration. For any other node, tell Black Hole

- **the RPC endpoint**: the address of the RPC interface of the node. The defaults are the ones of Bitcoin Core, `http://localhost:8332` on mainnet.
- **the RPC credentials**: `rpcuser:rpcpassword`, or `cookiefile=` followed by the path of the cookie file of the node. Left empty, the cookie file in the default data folder of Bitcoin Core (`~/.bitcoin`) is used.

*Connect to Node* of [the settings](#the-settings) asks for the two. *Verify Connection* there checks them with the node. *Done* does too and, if the node answers and is on the right network, works with it right away.

The same settings are `BitcoinRpcEndPoint` and `BitcoinRpcCredentialString` in `Config.json`.

Recovering a wallet scans every block since the activation of Taproot unless you tell the height the wallet was created at (*Advanced Recovery Options*). On mainnet the full scan takes hours.

A wallet watches 210 unused addresses after the last one that was paid to (its gap limit). A recovered wallet does not find a payment whose address follows more unused addresses than that until the gap limit is raised, in *Advanced Recovery Options* or later with *Resync Wallet*, which is behind *Configure Wallet* in the settings. Addresses stay unused when they were generated with *New Address* and never paid to. Coinjoin rounds that fail leave change addresses unused too, but they do not take the wallet beyond its gap limit, see [Coinjoin](#coinjoin).

## Private broadcast

Black Hole hands every transaction of the wallet to your node and takes the node to broadcast it privately. Bitcoin Core does that since version 31 with `privatebroadcast=1` in its `bitcoin.conf`, for which the node needs a way to Tor (`proxy=` or `onion=`) or to I2P (`i2psam=`). Such a node does not announce the transaction to the nodes that it is connected to, which would tell them that the transaction came from yours. It sends it over a few short-lived connections through Tor or I2P instead, and takes it into its own mempool only when it hears it back from the network, like a transaction of anybody else.

Black Hole does not check that your node does this, and it cannot ask the node for it: it assumes it. A node without the setting announces the transaction itself, as its own.

What follows from the way such a node works:

- **A transaction is on its way for some seconds.** The node needs them to open its connections and to hear the transaction back. The wallet lists the transaction at once.
- **A payment from the change of a payment that was just sent waits for that one.** Until the node has heard a transaction back it knows nothing of what the transaction pays out, and it refuses a transaction that spends from it. Black Hole hands the second transaction over again every second until the node takes it. A node that has not heard the first one back after a minute does not get through to Tor or I2P; Black Hole says so then, and the second payment is to be sent again later.
- **A speed-up or a cancellation stops what it replaces.** If the node is still sending the transaction that is replaced, Black Hole tells it to stop, so that it does not go on sending both.
- **The node keeps what it is sending in its memory.** A transaction that it has not heard back when it is restarted is gone, and Black Hole goes on listing it as pending. Cancelling it in the *History*, or a speed-up that replaces it, puts something on its way again.

## Data

Black Hole keeps its data in `~/.blackhole/client`. The folder button in the top right corner of the window shows that folder in the file manager; the wallet, the config file and the logs are in it. It does not read or change the data of a Wasabi Wallet installation (`~/.walletwasabi`), and its Tor listens on other ports (37250 and 37251), so both can run on the same computer.

Started with `--loglevel=debug`, Black Hole writes into `Logs.txt` what the wallet asks the node for while it synchronizes: every block when it is asked for, when it arrives and when it is processed. That tells what a wallet that seems to be stuck is waiting for. The file starts anew when it reaches 10 MB, which is about 25,000 blocks; with `--loglevel=trace` it has no limit.

## Coinjoin

*Connect to Coordinator* of [the settings](#the-settings) sets the coordinator, without a restart. The coordinator of this repository (`WalletWasabi.Coordinator`) speaks the same protocol as the wallet. The coordinators of Wasabi Wallet expect the "ready to sign" message that Black Hole does not send, so do not count on them.

Coinjoins are started by hand. Tick coins on the *Coins* tab, press *Coinjoin* and open the *WabiSabi* tab: *Play* starts one round with the ticked coins, and when the round is over the coinjoin is stopped; the box tells how the round ended. The round gets the coins that are ticked at the moment you press *Play*, wherever they come from and however often they have been in a coinjoin, but ten at most: if more are ticked, the ten largest go into the round and the others stay ticked for a later one. A ticked coin also stays out if it cannot be registered at that time, for example because it is unconfirmed or banned by the coordinator, or if the round does not take its amount. While the round is under way its coins carry the coinjoin sign in the list, and the ticks are free for something else: what you tick or untick then does not change the round. The coins that a coinjoin gives you are new coins and unticked: tick them to submit them into another round. With no coin ticked, *Play* tells that no coin is ticked and nothing else happens. Nor does the wallet call a coin private, semi-private or non-private, and the preview of a transaction does not judge what the transaction gives away: which coins it spends together, how many payments it makes, how much of the balance it takes and whether it has change is yours to decide. The preview only has a sign, with what it means, when the transaction spends coins that are unconfirmed or in a coinjoin round, because of what that may do to the transaction.

Every round gets addresses that no round had before. A round that fails has shown the addresses of your outputs to the coordinator and, once its transaction was up for signing, to everybody in it; an address that came back in a later round would tell that the same wallet was in both. So the addresses of a failed round are left behind: the blame round that follows a round that not everybody signed gets new ones, and so does the next coinjoin after a round whose transaction could not be broadcast. The wallet file knows that an address is taken before the coordinator gets to see it, so closing the wallet in the middle of a round does not bring it back either. A wallet that is recovered from its backup has no such record: nothing on the chain tells which addresses a failed round took, so it may give them out again. Only the address of a payment that you queued for a coinjoin stays the same from round to round, because it is not the wallet's to change.

There is one exception, for the sake of the gap limit. A round that fails leaves up to ten change addresses unused, and a wallet that is recovered from its backup watches 210 unused change addresses after the last one that was paid to. So once 200 change addresses in a row have gone to rounds that failed, the rounds after them share the last ten of the 210: these ten come back from round to round. That ends when something is paid to one of them, by a round that succeeds or by the change of a payment. The wallet counts anew from the address that was paid to, and every round gets new addresses again. While rounds have had all ten, the change of a payment goes to the last of them, since an address beyond the 210 is one that a recovered wallet would not find.

The lists show every coin with its *anonymity points*. An output of a transaction is *matched* if another output of the same transaction has the same amount. What a matched output is worth depends on how many outputs have its amount: 1 point if they are three or more, half a point if they are a pair. An output that is alone with its amount is worth nothing. A transaction is worth what its outputs are worth together, but not more than its inputs, where every input counts what it was worth as an output of the transaction that created it: the lower of the two sums counts. So three equal outputs are worth 3 points, a pair is worth 1 point in all, and a transaction that spends one coin out of a set of three and one out of a pair is worth 1.5 points at most. The matched outputs of the transaction carry its points for as long as they are unspent; its other outputs, such as change, carry none. Nothing is inherited or averaged: the points of a coin do not depend on the points of the coins that paid for it, and they are gone when the coin is spent. A first coinjoin of coins that were never matched is therefore worth 0 points, and so is an ordinary payment. To count the inputs the wallet asks your node for the transactions that created them, also for the inputs of others. A coin shows *?* until the node has answered; a pruned node may no longer have the blocks that it takes.

Matched outputs are also what the wallet takes as unlinked. A coin that one of your transactions makes counts as linked to the coins that the transaction spent, unless it is a matched output of that transaction; change, whose amount occurs once, stays linked. When the wallet picks the coins of a payment out of the ticked ones, it takes confirmed coins before unconfirmed ones, then as few unlinked coins as the amount needs, then the coins closest to the amount. The preview of a payment does not warn about the coins that it spends together: they are among the ones that you ticked. The points of a coin do not make the wallet prefer it or hold it back.

A coinjoin splits your coins into standard amounts. What is left and is too small for an output of its own goes to the miners. *Dust forfeit limit* on the *Connect to Coordinator* page of the settings limits that: if a coinjoin would leave more than the limit, the leftover is added to one of your outputs instead. You keep the money, but that output has a non-standard amount and gains less privacy than the others. The limit is 0.00005 BTC unless you change it, and it cannot be set above 0.0001 BTC. Because of this limit the wallet also joins rounds whose smallest output is large ("whale rounds"), which Wasabi Wallet refuses from 0.0001 BTC on: what such a round cannot put into an output is kept the same way. If a round cannot keep the limit at all, the wallet gives up that round instead of signing it.

## ZeroLink rounds

A ZeroLink round is a coinjoin of the strictest kind: every participant puts in one coin, all the coins hold the same amount, and every participant gets one output, all of the same amount again. There is no change, and nothing in the transaction tells which inputs belong to one wallet, because no wallet has more than one. The rounds use the credentials and the phases of WabiSabi, not the blind signatures that the ZeroLink rounds of Wasabi Wallet 1 had, and the same coordinator holds them.

- **Input.** The amount of a round is a standard denomination: one of the amounts that WabiSabi coinjoins make their outputs of. In satoshis that is a power of 2, a power of 3 or twice that, or 1, 2 or 5 times a power of 10, for example 0.1 BTC, 0.01594323 BTC or 0.00131072 BTC. A round takes Taproot coins of exactly its amount.
- **Output.** Every output holds the denomination less the fee of a transaction with one input and one output: 111 vB at the fee rate of the round, rounded up to a whole satoshi. That is 57.5 vB for the input, 43 vB for the output and 10.5 vB for what a transaction has besides those. The inputs of a round are signed with the short signature of Taproot, 64 bytes, which is what makes an input 57.5 vB and every input look like the others; the coordinator takes no other signature. At 5 sat/vB a coin of 0.1 BTC comes out as 0.09999445 BTC. A round has that overhead once, so its transaction pays a little more than the fee rate: with n participants (n − 1) × 10.5 vB more, about 9% of the fee. In return the amount of the outputs is known before the round starts.
- **Which rounds there are.** The coordinator holds ZeroLink rounds for the denominations that `ZeroLinkDenominations` lists in its `Config.json`, for example `"ZeroLinkDenominations": ["0.1", "0.01"]`, one round of each at a time. The list is empty unless you fill it, and a coin of a denomination that is not listed finds no round. The coordinator does not start with a list that it cannot read, or with an amount in it that is not a standard denomination. The numbers of inputs and the timeouts of a ZeroLink round are the ones of the other rounds of the coordinator. A denomination that the fee would eat, or leave dust of, has no round until the fee rate comes down.
- **Taking part.** Tick a coin of a standard amount on the *Coins* tab, press *Coinjoin*, open the *ZeroLink* tab and press *Play*. One coin goes into one round: if several ticked coins have rounds, a coin of the largest amount goes, and the others stay ticked. With no ticked coin of a standard amount the box says so at once. If the coordinator has no round of the amount, the box says after ten minutes that no round was found. A wallet has one coinjoin at a time: while a round is under way the box shows that round with its *Stop*, whatever tab is open, so another round cannot be started until it is over. In the daemon, `startzerolink` starts a ZeroLink round with one of the coins that were chosen with `selectforcoinjoin`.
- **What the wallet checks.** Before it signs, the wallet checks the transaction against these rules: every input holds the denomination, every output holds the denomination less the fee, there are no more outputs than inputs and nothing else, and the fee is paid. The coordinator adds no output of its own. If a participant registers an input and then no output, the transaction has one output less; that participant does not sign, and the others go on to a blame round of the same denomination.
- **Afterwards.** What a ZeroLink round gives is not a standard amount, as the fee is missing, so the coin cannot go into another ZeroLink round as it is. A WabiSabi coinjoin makes standard amounts of it again. All the outputs of a ZeroLink round have the same amount. With three or more of them every output is worth a point, so the transaction is worth as many anonymity points as its inputs were worth as outputs before, see [Coinjoin](#coinjoin); a round of two is a pair, and worth one point at most.

## Fidelity bonds

A fidelity bond is coins that are locked until the chain reaches a block height. A maker shows one to prove that it has something to lose, which makes it costly to pose as many makers. *Lock Coins* on the *Maker* tab of the dialog that *Coinjoin* on the *Coins* tab opens locks a bond, tells about it, and redeems it. Every offer of the wallet carries a proof of the bond, see [The market for anonymity points](#the-market-for-anonymity-points). The bonds are not the ones of JoinMarket, which does not understand them: they are Taproot outputs, and they unlock at a block height instead of a date.

- **Format.** A bond is a Taproot output that can be spent by one script only: `<unlock height> OP_CHECKLOCKTIMEVERIFY OP_DROP <public key> OP_CHECKSIG`. Its internal key is the point that BIP341 names for outputs without a key path (`50929b74c1a04954b78b4b6035e97a5e078a5a0f28ec96d547bfee9ace803ac0`). Nobody has the private key of that point, so whoever is shown the script can check that there is no way around the timelock. On the chain a bond looks like any other Taproot output until it is redeemed.
- **Lock time.** You name for how many blocks the coins are locked: 2,016, about two weeks, unless you type another number. The bond unlocks at the height of the chain plus that number; it is the blocks that decide, not the calendar. A bond can be redeemed once the chain has reached the height: the transaction that redeems it is valid from the next block on. The longest lock is 525,600 blocks, which is about ten years. Locked coins cannot be spent before that by anyone, and there is no way to undo it.
- **Keys.** The key of a bond is `m/86'/0'/0'/2/h` on mainnet, where `h` is the unlock height. It is a key of the account of the wallet, and every height has its own.
- **Locking.** On *Lock Coins*, tick coins in the list, name the amount, the lock time and the fee rate, and press *Bond*. Only coins with at least one anonymity point can be picked; the others are greyed out. The strategy triangle proposes how much of the wallet to lock and picks coins for it, the largest first; you can pick others and type another amount. Before anything is sent, a question states the amount that will be locked, the lock ("Locked for 2,016 blocks (2 weeks)") and the fee rate with the fee of the transaction as the wallet built it. The wallet spends as few of the picked coins as the amount takes. When the amount is everything that the picked coins hold, the mining fee is taken out of the bond.
- **Standing.** Once a bond exists, *Lock Coins* tells about it in place of the form: its amount, for how many blocks it was locked, how many blocks are left, and its rank and percentile among the bonds of the makers of the order book, by amount, the largest being rank 1. The bond is on top of the list of coins with a padlock and the blocks that it is still locked for. While it exists, the split of the wallet between bond and offers is what the bond makes it.
- **Balance and history.** The coins in a bond belong to the wallet and are part of its balance, though it cannot spend them yet. The balance at the top of the window is everything that the wallet owns, in one amount. In the history, locking and unlocking have icons of their own, and their amount is what they changed in the balance: the mining fee, when the coins came from the wallet or go back to it. The details of the transaction tell the amount that was locked or unlocked.
- **Redeeming.** Once the chain has reached the unlock height, *Redeem* on *Lock Coins* pays the bond back to the wallet, to a new address of it, less the mining fee.
- **Recovery.** There are too many heights to watch the addresses of all of them, so the wallet watches the ones that it gave out, and the wallet file remembers them. The recovery words do not tell them. A wallet that is recovered, or told to *Resync Wallet*, therefore looks for a bond at every height that it could have chosen, from the first block that it goes through to ten years above the chain. Getting ready for that takes about a minute before the first block is processed. This finds the bonds that are in the blocks. A bond that another copy of the wallet made after the recovery is found by resyncing again.
- **Privacy.** A bond that is shown to others is public, and so is the history of the coins in it. Every offer of the wallet shows the same bond, so the offers of one wallet can be told to belong together by it.

## The market for anonymity points

A maker offers coins that a taker may put into a coinjoin of the taker's making. The coinjoin destroys the [anonymity points](#coinjoin) of the coins that go into it, and the coins that come out of it have the points of the new transaction: it swaps the points of a maker's coins for others. A maker that has more points afterwards pays the taker its *buy rate* for each of them; a maker that has fewer is paid its *sell rate* for each by the taker. The taker pays the mining fee of what it brings and of what the makers contribute for it; a maker pays for the inputs and outputs that it adds for its own sake.

The *Maker* and *Taker* tabs of the dialog that *Coinjoin* on the *Coins* tab opens are the two sides of this market. **What works:** locking a bond, posting offers and taking them back, reading and filtering the offers of others with their bonds checked by your node, and planning the transaction with what it would cost. **What does not exist yet:** the transaction itself. How a taker reaches the makers that it picked, what a maker checks before it signs, and how the fees and the settlement are taken out of the outputs is not designed yet, so *Sign & broadcast* on the *Taker* tab cannot be pressed, on any network. Until then an offer moves no coins.

Both tabs have three columns: a strategy triangle with rows of sliders under it on the left, a table in the middle, and two panels on the right. A row under a triangle has a box for typing an exact value (Enter takes it, Escape gives it up) and a bar that is also a slider. Clicking or dragging in a triangle, or moving its marker with the arrow keys, sets the rows under it at once; moving one of them by hand takes the marker out of the triangle.

### Maker

- **Strategy.** The corners are *Earn AP*, *Earn sats* and *Remix often*. Each stands for a share of the wallet that goes into the bond (50%, 30%, 70%) and for where the three rates of an offer sit among the offers of the book: how many of the makers the fee rate and the sell rate undercut, and how many the buy rate exceeds. Between the corners the figures are blended. The split is live on *Lock Coins* while there is no bond, and the rates on *List Offers*. With an empty book the triangle has no rates to propose, and they are typed.
- **Coins.** The list has the coins of the wallet with their anonymity points and amounts. Its heads sort it by amount or by points, and the head of the first column switches between outputs and addresses. The triangle picks coins along the order of the list, for an offer whether they have points or not; you can tick others. Sorted by points, the list has the coins with the most points first.
- **List Offers.** An offer has an amount, a minimum fee rate in sat/vB, and a sell rate and a buy rate in sats per anonymity point. The sell rate cannot be lower than the buy rate: such a maker could be emptied by giving it points and taking them back. The fee rate is between 0.1 and 10,000 sat/vB. Three coins of an offer count: the three largest picked coins that come from different transactions, whether they have anonymity points or not. They are the most that the amount can be, and the button tells the points of the offer ("Offer 8.4 AP"): the mean of the points of the first three picked coins, weighted by their amounts, in which a coin without points counts with none. An offer whose coins have no points has none to sell. It lists a sell rate of 0, the box of the sell rate cannot be filled while that is so, and its buy rate, which is what it pays for the points that it gains, is not held against its sell rate. A picked coin without points is yellow. One from the transaction of a larger picked coin is red and one beyond the first three is orange: these two only add inputs that you pay for. An offer can have up to 20 coins. A posted offer is listed under the form with *Cancel*; its coins cannot be picked again, the next three are picked, and the rates stay for the next offer. Several offers stand next to each other.
- **Where an offer goes.** To the Nostr relays that you connected to with *Connect to Nostr* of [the settings](#the-settings), which can be relays that users of Black Hole run themselves, see [Your own Nostr relay](#your-own-nostr-relay). A new installation has `wss://relay.primal.net`, `wss://relay.damus.io` and `wss://nos.lol`; with no relay in the list no offer can be posted. Like the other settings they can be given at the start instead, with `--nostrrelays=wss://one.example;wss://two.example`. The relays are reached through Tor, unless Tor is turned off. These and the coordinator of your coinjoins are the only servers that Black Hole talks to besides your node.
- **What an offer tells.** It is an event of the kind for the discovery of coinjoins (15750, a [draft](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/384)) with the tags `type` = `BlackHoleJoinMarket` and `network` = `mainnet`, `signet` or `regtest`. Its content is JSON, for example `{"minFeeRate":2.5,"buy":100,"sell":150.5,"liquidity":50000000,"points":2.5,"inputs":3,"extraInputs":1}`: the fee rate, the two rates, the amount in sats, the points, how many coins count and how many more there are. With a bond it also has `"bond":{"txid":…,"vout":…,"unlockHeight":…,"key":…,"signature":…}`. The third tag of that kind, `endpoint`, is left out: there is no place yet where a taker reaches a maker.
- **What it does not tell.** Which coins they are, and what each of them holds. Whoever reads the relays can still search the chain for coins of that amount. Every offer is signed with a new key, and a maker is called by two words that the key of its offer gives it ("gilded-raven"), the same for everybody who reads the book. Without a bond, two offers cannot be told to come from the same wallet by their keys; with a bond they can, by the bond.
- **The proof of the bond.** The largest bond of the wallet that is in a block and still locked stands behind every offer. The proof names the output and the unlock height of the bond and the key of its script, and carries a BIP340 signature by that key over SHA256(SHA256(tag) ‖ SHA256(tag) ‖ key of the offer ‖ transaction id ‖ output index ‖ unlock height), with the tag `BlackHole/FidelityBondProof` and the two numbers as four bytes, the lowest first. The script of a bond commits to its key and to its height, so whoever reads the offer works the script out and asks their own node whether an unspent output in a block has it. The signature is over the key of the offer, so the proof of one offer is of no use in another.
- **How long an offer stands.** Until you cancel it, until one of its coins is spent, or until you close Black Hole. Readers take an offer that is older than 15 minutes for gone, so a standing offer is posted again every 10 minutes, with the bond looked at anew. The kind is replaceable, which means that a relay keeps only the newest event of a key: an offer is taken back by an event of its key that tells nothing. Offers are not stored: after a restart there is none until you post one. An offer keeps no coin from being spent or coinjoined.
- **When no relay can be reached.** An offer that no relay took is not made, and the form tells why. A standing offer that cannot be posted again says so on its card, and is tried again every minute.

### Taker

- **The order book.** The offers that your Nostr relays have, read while the tab or the *Order Book* tab shows: at once and then every minute, every time on Tor circuits of their own. Of every key the newest event counts, if it is signed by that key, tells an offer for this network and is not older than 15 minutes. Makers whose bond your node finds come first, with what the bond holds; a maker whose proof names nothing that the node has, or cannot be read, is listed among the *Unbonded Makers*. The columns are the fee rate, the spread (sell rate less buy rate), the sell rate, the buy rate, the liquidity and the bond, and their heads sort the table. A maker whose offer has no points has none to sell: it is listed with a sell rate of 0, whatever it says, so its spread is below zero. Relays that do not answer are no empty book: what was read before stays while it counts, and the tab says who did not answer.
- **Filters.** Each of the six has a slider from *any* to the best value of the book: every step drops one more maker. A typed value is the limit as it was typed. A maker that a filter cuts is greyed out and cannot be ticked, and its cells that fail are dimmed further. Under the filters the tab counts the makers of the book and the liquidity that pass; your own offers are rows of the table and are not counted.
- **Strategy.** The corners are *Maximize Privacy*, *Minimize Costs* and *Maximize Speed*. Each says what share of the makers with a bond the filters of the bond, the fee rate, the sell rate and the buy rate admit; the filter of the spread follows from the two rates, and the liquidity is yours to set. The tab opens with the marker in the middle.
- **Picking makers.** A click on a row ticks it. The box in the head of the table ticks every maker that passes, and from then on the selection follows the filters, until a single row is ticked or unticked. A filter that moves unticks the makers that no longer pass.
- **Your coins.** The taker brings the coins that are ticked on the *Coins* tab. An offer of this wallet is in the book with a *you* tag, and ticking it makes its coins what the taker brings instead.
- **Transaction Builder.** For the picked makers: the anonymity points of the transaction, the participants, the liquidity, the inputs that count, the points of the outputs, and the values of the outputs with how many outputs have each and whose they are. A value that three or more outputs have is white, a pair is yellow, and a value that one output is alone with is red. The makers' coins are split into values that they share with each other and with the taker, by the planner of the design; the plan is made anew whenever the selection changes.
- **Price Calculator.** What the transaction would cost: the mining fee line by line (11 vB of overhead, 57 vB for an input, 43 vB for an output on a shared value, 100 vB for one that is alone with its value) at the highest minimum fee rate of the picked makers, and who pays each line; then for every maker the points that it had, the points that it gains or loses, the rate that applies and the sats; and what the taker pays or receives in all.
- **What the plan counts.** Up to three inputs of every maker count towards the points of the transaction and none of the taker's, and an offer tells its points as one number. That is how the market is designed so far; what a coin of the wallet shows after a transaction is still counted as [Coinjoin](#coinjoin) describes, from every input.

### Sample data

*Sample data*, at the right of the tabs, shows both sides with the examples that the market was designed with in place of your wallet and your relays: 250 makers and a wallet of 16 coins. Offers and bonds that you make there stay on the screen and go nowhere. With it the tab opens as the design does: the marker in the middle of the taker's triangle admits 48 of the 250 makers, with 76.79 of 418.91 BTC.
